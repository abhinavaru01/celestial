# Positioning & Differentiation

## The one-line pitch
**Project Compression teaches grades 6–12 in half the time by removing what conventional curricula waste — not by making kids study longer.** It graduates students who are board-equivalent, competitive-exam-ready, and able to build real software.

## The problem we attack
Legacy curricula (NCERT and international boards alike) are structurally redundant: the same concept — ratio, motion, the mole — is taught three or four times across grades, each time nearly from scratch, because sequencing is tied to age rather than to knowledge dependencies. The result is ~7 years of school to reach a readiness a well-sequenced path reaches in ~3. Coaching culture then bolts on 1–2 *more* years for competitive exams, inflating hours and driving burnout.

## Our wedge: compression by structure, not by intensity
The differentiator is the **knowledge dependency graph**. Because every topic declares its prerequisites (cross-subject included), we can:
- teach each concept **once** and *reuse* it everywhere (speed is a rate — never re-taught),
- **interleave** subjects so math arrives exactly when physics needs it (no waiting, no re-teaching),
- gate on **mastery**, so no student is held back by age-based lockstep or pushed forward unprepared.

Daily study hours go **down**, calendar time is roughly **halved**, and retention goes **up** (spacing + interleaving are the best-evidenced learning techniques). That combination — faster *and* less intense *and* better-retained — is what no incumbent offers.

## Competitive landscape

| Player | What they do | What they miss (our edge) |
|---|---|---|
| **Byju's / Vedantu / Unacademy** | Video lessons + test prep mapped to existing boards | They *accelerate delivery* of the same redundant syllabus; they don't restructure it. No dependency-driven compression, no mastery gating, weak "build real things" outcome. |
| **Khan Academy** | Free, high-quality, mastery-flavoured, board-agnostic | Broad but not *compressed* or *interleaved by design*; not exam-outcome engineered for JEE/NEET; CS is peripheral. |
| **CBSE/ICSE/IB schools** | Accreditation + structure | The very redundancy and lockstep we remove; ~7-year timeline; burnout-heavy exam culture. |
| **Coaching institutes (Allen, FIITJEE)** | Deep exam prep | Bolt-on, separate, intense, expensive; assume the school years happened elsewhere. We fold readiness into the core. |

## Why this can become a global standard
- **Board-independent but board-equivalent:** the coverage matrix proves nothing essential is lost, so it ports across CBSE/ICSE/IB/US markets without rebuilding content — only the equivalence mapping changes.
- **Content-as-data + repeatable pattern:** every topic follows one six-layer contract, so the curriculum scales to new subjects, languages, and regions as a *process*, not bespoke authoring.
- **Outcome credibility:** the promise is measurable (board-equivalent + exam-ready + ships software), which is what regulators, schools, and parents can be sold on.
- **The CS "builder" outcome** differentiates graduates in a way no board currently does — a marketable, future-facing signal.

## Target segments (initial)
1. **Ambitious self-learners / homeschoolers** (D2C) — the beachhead: they feel the redundancy pain most and can adopt without institutional buy-in.
2. **Micro-schools & progressive schools** (B2B) — teacher tools + dependency map + coverage proof make adoption defensible.
3. **Later:** mainstream schools and international expansion once outcome data exists.

## Business-model notes (for a later full plan)
- Freemium content, paid mastery-tracking + competitive track + teacher dashboards.
- B2B per-seat licensing for institutions (includes teacher tools, roster analytics — the trigger for the backend build).
- The moat compounds: more content authored on the same contract → deeper dependency graph → harder to replicate the compression.
