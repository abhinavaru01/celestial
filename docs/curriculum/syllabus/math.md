# Mathematics — Tier-by-Tier Syllabus

**Sequencing principle:** classical school math teaches arithmetic → fractions → percentages → ratio → algebra as separate yearly units, re-teaching each from scratch. We collapse them into one strand — **multiplicative & proportional reasoning** — taught once, deeply, then *used* everywhere (physics rates, chemistry moles, probability, finance). Algebra starts in F1 as "generalized arithmetic," not as a grade-8 event. Calculus intuition arrives in I2, two years earlier than convention, because physics needs it and because the *ideas* (rate of change, accumulation) are not intrinsically harder than the algebra students already do — only the formalism is, and we defer the formalism.

## F1
1. **Number sense & estimation** — place value, negative numbers, orders of magnitude, mental math, estimation discipline.
2. **Fractions & decimals as one system** — a single model (the number line + division) instead of three disconnected procedure sets.
3. **Ratio, proportion & percentage** *(built — proof-of-model topic)* — the highest-leverage topic in the program; explicit rate thinking.
4. **Algebra as generalized arithmetic** — variables, expressions, evaluating; equations as statements to be kept balanced.
5. **Linear equations in one variable** — solving, modeling word problems directly with algebra (skipping years of arithmetic-only word-problem drill).
6. **Geometry I: angles, triangles, congruence intuition** — with construction; proofs deferred, *reasoning* not.
7. **Data & graphs I** — reading, drawing, misleading-graph literacy; feeds every science strand immediately.

## F2
1. **Exponents & scientific notation** — needed by chemistry (moles) and physics immediately.
2. **Linear relationships & the coordinate plane** — y = mx + c as the mathematics of *rates* (direct bridge from F1.3).
3. **Systems of linear equations** — substitution/elimination, modeling.
4. **Polynomials & factoring basics.**
5. **Geometry II: similarity, Pythagoras, areas & volumes** — similarity taught as "ratio applied to shapes" (explicit reuse of F1.3).
6. **Probability & statistics I** — probability as proportion; mean/median/spread.
7. **Integer number theory basics** — divisibility, primes, gcd/lcm (also serves CS algorithms).

## I1
1. **Quadratics** — factoring, formula, graphs, modeling.
2. **Coordinate geometry** — lines, circles, distance/section formulas.
3. **Trigonometry I** — right-triangle trig + unit-circle intuition early (physics vectors need it in I1).
4. **Sequences & series** — AP/GP; compound growth (links to finance & biology populations).
5. **Geometry III: circles, formal proof** — deductive proof as a topic in itself (transfers to CS correctness reasoning).
6. **Statistics II** — grouped data, dispersion; reading real studies (links to biology).

## I2
1. **Functions as objects** — notation, composition, inverses, transformations of graphs.
2. **Trigonometry II** — identities, equations, non-right triangles.
3. **Calculus intuition: derivatives** — rate of change made exact; taught alongside I2 physics kinematics so each motivates the other.
4. **Calculus intuition: integrals** — accumulation; area under rate curves.
5. **Exponentials & logarithms** — growth/decay across chemistry (pH), biology (populations), CS (complexity).
6. **Combinatorics & probability II** — counting principles, conditional probability.

## A1
1. **Calculus formalized: limits, differentiation rules, applications** (maxima/minima, curve sketching, related rates).
2. **Integration techniques & applications.**
3. **Vectors & 3-D geometry** — shared spine with A1 physics.
4. **Matrices & determinants** — solving systems; transformations (links to CS graphics/data).
5. **Complex numbers.**
6. **Permutations, combinations, binomial theorem.**

## A2
1. **Differential equations (introductory)** — modeling; direct physics reuse.
2. **Probability distributions & statistics III** — binomial/normal, inference intuition.
3. **Advanced coordinate geometry: conics.**
4. **Advanced problem-solving portfolio** — multi-topic, competition-style structured problems (capstone strand).
5. **Mathematical modeling capstone** — one real modeling project, written up (links to English argumentation + CS implementation).

## Why this sequence is faster
- **One proportional-reasoning spine** replaces ~2.5 years of scattered fraction/percent/ratio re-teaching (grades 5–8 in legacy curricula).
- **Algebra from day one** removes the arithmetic-only word-problem detour of grades 6–7.
- **Calculus intuition in I2** lets A-tier physics and math run concurrently instead of physics waiting for math (the classic grade-11 bottleneck).
- **Interleaving:** every statistics/probability topic is exercised inside science topics the same sub-level, so review is embedded in use, not scheduled as separate revision.
