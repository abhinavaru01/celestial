# Biology — Tier-by-Tier Syllabus

**Sequencing principle:** biology is the subject most damaged by rote learning. Legacy curricula present it as vocabulary lists. We organize it around **a few big explanatory models** — cell theory, evolution by natural selection, the flow of energy and information — and hang every fact on the model that explains it. Facts become predictable consequences instead of items to memorize. Quantitative biology (genetics ratios, population growth, rates) is interleaved with the matching math, so biology also reinforces proportional and exponential reasoning.

## F1
1. **What is life? Characteristics & classification** — organizing principle, not a taxonomy list.
2. **The cell** — cell theory, structure, plant vs animal, microscopy.
3. **Nutrition & the human digestive system** — energy intake (links to Physics energy accounting).
4. **Living things & their environment I** — food chains as energy flow.

## F2
1. **Cells to organisms** — tissues, organs, systems (organizing hierarchy).
2. **Human physiology I** — respiration, circulation, transport (as flow systems).
3. **Plant biology I** — photosynthesis as energy capture, transport in plants.
4. **Reproduction** — asexual/sexual, human reproductive system.
5. **Health & disease** — pathogens, immunity, prevention.

## I1
1. **Cell processes** — diffusion, osmosis, active transport (uses concentration, Chem I1.4).
2. **Enzymes** — catalysis, rate factors (links to Chem I1.5 rates).
3. **Respiration & photosynthesis (biochemical)** — quantitative energy budgets.
4. **Human physiology II** — nervous & endocrine coordination, homeostasis as feedback control.
5. **Ecology & ecosystems** — energy pyramids, nutrient cycles, populations (intro).

## I2
1. **Genetics I: inheritance** — Mendel, monohybrid/dihybrid — genetics *is* probability (co-taught with Math I2.6).
2. **DNA & the molecular basis of inheritance** — replication, the central dogma (information flow model).
3. **Evolution & natural selection** — the unifying theory; evidence, mechanisms.
4. **Population biology** — exponential/logistic growth (uses Math I2.5 exponentials).
5. **Human systems capstone** — integrated case studies.

## A1
1. **Cell biology at depth** — membrane transport, cell cycle, division (mitosis/meiosis).
2. **Molecular genetics** — transcription, translation, gene regulation.
3. **Biochemistry** — biomolecules (shared with Chem A2), metabolic pathways overview.
4. **Plant physiology at depth** — photosynthesis/respiration mechanisms, plant hormones.
5. **Human physiology III** — detailed systems (digestive, circulatory, excretory, nervous).

## A2
1. **Genetics II** — linkage, mutation, genetic disorders, population genetics (Hardy-Weinberg — reuses probability).
2. **Biotechnology** — recombinant DNA, PCR, applications, ethics (links to English argumentation).
3. **Evolution at depth** — speciation, molecular evidence, phylogenetics.
4. **Ecology & environment at depth** — biodiversity, conservation, human impact (data-driven, reuses Statistics).
5. **Human health & disease at depth** — immunity, reproduction, biotech in medicine.
6. **Investigative biology portfolio** — experimental design, data analysis (capstone; reuses Statistics II/III).

## Why this sequence is faster
- **Model-first, not list-first:** organizing around cell theory / evolution / information flow means facts are *derived and predicted*, collapsing rote memorization that consumes most legacy biology time.
- **Genetics interleaved with probability** (I2) — students learn one skill that serves two subjects; ratios in inheritance stop being a separate hurdle.
- **Population growth interleaved with exponentials**, biochemistry shared with chemistry — no duplicate teaching of the same quantitative tools.
