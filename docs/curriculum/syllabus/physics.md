# Physics — Tier-by-Tier Syllabus

**Sequencing principle:** legacy curricula teach physics three times (grades 6–8 descriptively, 9–10 semi-quantitatively, 11–12 properly). We teach each idea **once, quantitatively from the start**, at the moment the math strand has just delivered the needed tool. Kinematics lands right after ratio/rates (F1); mechanics deepens when trig and calculus intuition arrive. The descriptive pass is deleted — that alone reclaims ~2 years of the physics timeline.

## F1
1. **Measurement, units & estimation** — SI, unit conversion as ratio reasoning (explicit reuse of Math F1.3), significant figures, Fermi estimates.
2. **Motion: speed, velocity & acceleration** *(built — proof-of-model topic)* — rates applied to the physical world; graphs of motion (reuses Math F1.7).
3. **Force & Newton's laws (introductory)** — quantitative from the start: F = ma with one-dimensional problems.
4. **Energy I** — work, kinetic/potential energy, conservation as an accounting principle.

## F2
1. **Pressure & density** — fluids, buoyancy (ratio reasoning again).
2. **Heat & temperature** — particle model, transfer, specific heat (calculation-based).
3. **Light I** — rays, reflection, refraction, lenses (uses F2 similarity geometry).
4. **Sound & waves I** — wave parameters as rates; frequency/wavelength/speed relation.
5. **Electricity I** — circuits, Ohm's law, series/parallel (uses linear equations).
6. **Magnetism I** — fields, electromagnets (descriptive + basic quantitative).

## I1
1. **Kinematics in two dimensions** — vectors introduced with Trig I (Math I1.3), projectiles.
2. **Newton's laws deepened** — friction, inclined planes, systems of bodies.
3. **Momentum & collisions.**
4. **Circular motion & gravitation** — orbits, g variation.
5. **Energy II** — power, efficiency, energy resources (quantitative).

## I2
1. **Kinematics with calculus** — position/velocity/acceleration as derivatives (co-taught with Math I2.3; each is the other's motivation).
2. **Oscillations (SHM intuition).**
3. **Waves II** — superposition, standing waves, Doppler.
4. **Electricity II** — EMF, internal resistance, Kirchhoff's laws, capacitors (introductory).
5. **Thermal physics** — gas laws (shared topic with Chemistry I2 — taught once, used by both strands).

## A1
1. **Mechanics at full rigor** — dynamics with calculus, work-energy theorem, rotational motion, rigid bodies.
2. **Gravitation (field treatment).**
3. **Fluids at A-level** — pressure, viscosity, Bernoulli (intro).
4. **Thermodynamics** — laws, heat engines, entropy (intro); kinetic theory (shared spine with Chemistry A1 physical chemistry).
5. **Electrostatics** — fields, potential, capacitance (uses A1 calculus + vectors).

## A2
1. **Current electricity & circuits at full rigor.**
2. **Magnetic effects & electromagnetic induction** — AC circuits (uses differential equations from Math A2.1).
3. **Optics at full rigor** — wave optics, interference, diffraction.
4. **Modern physics** — photoelectric effect, atomic models, nuclei, radioactivity, semiconductor basics.
5. **Experimental physics portfolio** — error analysis, real measurements, write-ups (capstone strand; reuses Statistics).

## Why this sequence is faster
- **No descriptive pass:** the grades 6–8 "physics without math" cycle is deleted; F-tier physics is already quantitative because F-tier math supplies rates and equations.
- **Just-in-time math:** every physics topic lands within weeks of its math prerequisite (rates→kinematics, trig→vectors, derivatives→calculus kinematics, ODEs→induction). No waiting, no re-teaching math inside physics.
- **Shared topics taught once:** gas laws and kinetic theory are single topics shared with chemistry rather than duplicated in both subjects.
