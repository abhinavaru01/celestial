# English — Tier-by-Tier Syllabus

**Sequencing principle:** standard school English plateaus at basic comprehension and formulaic essays. We treat English as **thinking made visible** — the skill of reading closely, reasoning in structured argument, and writing with precision — pitched a level above conventional school English. It is the connective subject: the writing skills are exercised directly in science lab write-ups, the argumentation in biology ethics and the math modeling capstone. Vocabulary is built by roots and reading volume, not weekly lists.

## F1
1. **Reading for meaning** — main idea, inference, distinguishing fact/opinion.
2. **Vocabulary by roots** — Greek/Latin roots and morphology (multiplies vocabulary instead of memorizing words one by one).
3. **Sentence craft** — grammar as a tool for clarity, not rules for their own sake; sentence combining.
4. **Paragraph architecture** — topic sentence, support, coherence.
5. **Narrative & descriptive writing.**

## F2
1. **Close reading of prose & poetry** — figurative language, tone, structure.
2. **Structured exposition** — the multi-paragraph explanatory essay.
3. **Summarizing & paraphrasing** — precision compression (directly serves note-taking across all subjects).
4. **Grammar for style** — clauses, punctuation for effect, active/passive control.
5. **Vocabulary II** — connotation, register, academic word list.

## I1
1. **Argument I** — claim, evidence, warrant; recognizing argument structure in texts.
2. **The persuasive/argumentative essay.**
3. **Analyzing non-fiction & media** — rhetoric, bias, source evaluation (serves science data-literacy).
4. **Literature I** — short stories & drama; theme and character analysis.
5. **Research & citation basics** — finding, evaluating, attributing sources.

## I2
1. **Argument II** — counterargument, rebuttal, logical fallacies.
2. **Comparative analysis** — synthesizing multiple texts.
3. **Literature II** — the novel; extended analytical essay.
4. **Formal & technical writing** — reports, lab-style write-ups (shared skill with science portfolios).
5. **Public speaking & structured discussion.**

## A1
1. **Rhetoric & style at depth** — voice, syntax variety, precision.
2. **Literary criticism** — critical lenses, close analysis of complex texts.
3. **The research paper** — thesis, literature synthesis, sustained argument (links to any subject capstone).
4. **Persuasion in the real world** — proposals, op-eds, evidence ethics.

## A2
1. **Advanced literary analysis** — poetry, drama, novel at exam depth.
2. **The extended essay / independent research writing** — capstone: a sustained, sourced, argued piece.
3. **Critical reasoning & comprehension at competitive-exam level** — dense unseen passages under time (serves aptitude sections of many entrance exams).
4. **Communication portfolio** — a body of polished writing across genres (capstone strand).

## Why this sequence is above standard school English
- **Roots-based vocabulary** and **volume reading** replace weekly word lists — vocabulary compounds.
- **Argument is a spine** taught explicitly across four sub-levels, not an occasional essay type; this is the transferable core that serves science, ethics, and competitive-exam reasoning.
- **Writing is exercised in other subjects** (lab write-ups, modeling capstone, biotech ethics), so practice volume is high without adding standalone hours — interleaving applied to language.
