# Chemistry — Tier-by-Tier Syllabus

**Sequencing principle:** chemistry's real bottleneck is not memorizing reactions — it is (1) the mole concept and (2) atomic/bonding models. Legacy curricula bury the mole in grade 11 after years of qualitative "types of reactions." We front-load quantitative thinking: particles → mole → stoichiometry early, so every later reaction is *calculated*, not memorized. The mole is proportional reasoning (Math F1.3) applied to particles, taught the moment exponents (Math F2.1) are available.

## F1
1. **Matter, particles & states** — particle model, changes of state, pure substances vs mixtures.
2. **Separation techniques** — filtration, distillation, chromatography (links to lab skills).
3. **Physical vs chemical change** — evidence of reaction, conservation of mass (accounting mindset, shared with Physics energy).

## F2
1. **Atomic structure I** — protons/neutrons/electrons, isotopes, atomic number.
2. **The periodic table as a map** — periods/groups as patterns, not a list to memorize.
3. **The mole concept** — Avogadro, molar mass; proportional reasoning + scientific notation applied (Math F1.3 + F2.1). **Keystone topic.**
4. **Chemical formulae & equations** — balancing as conservation accounting.
5. **Introductory stoichiometry** — mass-mole-mass calculations.

## I1
1. **Bonding I** — ionic, covalent, metallic; electron-dot structures.
2. **Reactions & equations deepened** — types, ionic equations, redox intro.
3. **Acids, bases & salts** — pH qualitatively, neutralization, titration intro.
4. **Solutions & concentration** — molarity (mole concept reused), dilution.
5. **Rates of reaction (qualitative + graphs)** — links to Physics/Math rate thinking.

## I2
1. **Atomic structure II** — electron configuration, quantum shells (intuitive).
2. **Bonding II** — shapes (VSEPR), polarity, intermolecular forces.
3. **Gas laws & kinetic theory** — shared topic with Physics I2 (taught once).
4. **Energetics (thermochemistry)** — enthalpy, Hess's law intro.
5. **Quantitative equilibrium intro** — reversible reactions, Le Chatelier.
6. **Introductory organic chemistry** — hydrocarbons, functional groups, naming.

## A1 (Physical + Inorganic emphasis)
1. **Chemical thermodynamics** — enthalpy, entropy, Gibbs free energy (shares thermo spine with Physics A1).
2. **Chemical kinetics** — rate laws, order, Arrhenius (uses logs, Math I2.5).
3. **Chemical equilibrium (quantitative)** — Kc, Kp, ICE tables.
4. **Ionic equilibrium** — pH calculations, buffers, solubility product.
5. **Electrochemistry** — cells, Nernst, electrolysis.
6. **Periodic properties & s/p-block chemistry.**

## A2 (Organic + Inorganic depth)
1. **Organic reaction mechanisms** — nucleophiles/electrophiles, key pathways (reasoned, not memorized).
2. **Functional-group chemistry** — alcohols, aldehydes/ketones, acids, amines.
3. **Biomolecules & polymers** — links to Biology A2.
4. **d-block & coordination chemistry.**
5. **Analytical & practical chemistry portfolio** — qualitative/quantitative analysis, error treatment (capstone; reuses Statistics).

## Why this sequence is faster
- **Mole front-loaded:** every subsequent reaction topic is quantitative from first contact — no "learn reactions qualitatively now, quantitatively later" double pass.
- **Periodic table taught as pattern**, cutting rote memorization to near zero; properties are *derived* from atomic structure.
- **Shared thermodynamics/gas-law spine** with physics eliminates duplicate teaching.
- **Organic as mechanism, not catalog:** reasoning transfers, so far fewer reactions need memorizing.
