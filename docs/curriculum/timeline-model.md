# Timeline Model — How 6+ Years Compresses to ≤3

**The claim:** board-equivalent grades 6–12 (conventionally ~7 school years) reaches completion in **≤3 years** of full-time study — *without* longer study days than a normal student, and with explicit anti-burnout guardrails.

**Where compression comes from (and where it does NOT):** it comes from **removing redundancy and interleaving**, per the locked decision. It does **not** come from inflating daily hours. This document shows the arithmetic honestly.

## The load assumption

- **Study week:** ~30 focused hours across 6 days (≈5 hrs/day) — *less* than a typical Indian board+coaching student, who often runs 8–12 hrs/day counting school + tuition. We are compressing calendar time by removing waste, and simultaneously *reducing* daily load.
- **Sub-level:** ~20 weeks each. Six sub-levels (F1→A2) = 120 weeks ≈ **2.5 years of study**, leaving ~0.5 year of slack for the mastery-based reality that some students repeat material. Ceiling: 3 years.
- **Weekly split (indicative, full-time):** Math ~6h, one Science strand rotating focus ~8h, English ~4h, CS ~6h, spaced review + assessment ~6h. Rotates so no single subject dominates a week.

## Where the ~4 reclaimed years come from

| Source of waste in legacy 6–12 | Reclaimed |
|---|---|
| **Repetition across grades** — ratio/percent taught in classes 5–8; motion taught descriptively then quantitatively then rigorously; the mole delayed then rushed. Teach each **once**. | ~2 years |
| **Descriptive→quantitative double-passes in science** — deleting the qualitative-only early pass (F-tier science is quantitative from the start because F-tier math supplies the tools). | ~1 year |
| **Waiting/lockstep** — physics waiting for math it needs; strong students held to class pace. Mastery-based + per-strand tiers remove the wait. | ~0.5–1 year |
| **Administrative & filler time** — exam drilling of format, revision-of-revision, non-objective content. | ~0.5 year |

These overlap somewhat, but conservatively total ~4 years reclaimed → ~7 years of objectives fit in ~2.5–3.

## Why this is NOT just "study faster / cram"

- Daily hours **go down**, not up — the guardrail against burnout.
- Compression is structural (curriculum redesign), not effort-based. A student isn't asked to learn any single topic faster than normal; they're asked to learn each topic **only once** and in an order where prerequisites are always fresh.
- **Interleaving + spaced review is built into the weekly plan**, so retention is *higher* than a cram model, not lower — directly supported by cognitive-science evidence on spacing and interleaving (see `decisions.md` research notes / `docs/business/research-notes.md`).

## Mastery-based means the timeline is a plan, not a promise

The ≤3 years is the expected path for a student meeting the mastery bars on the first or second attempt. Because progression is mastery-gated (`mastery-gating.md`):
- A faster student may finish in ~2 years.
- A student who needs more time on several gates takes longer — and that is *fine*; the guarantee is depth and correctness, not a fixed clock.
- Part-time learners (e.g., alongside conventional school) scale the calendar proportionally: ~15 hrs/week roughly doubles the calendar to ~5 years while preserving the structure.

## Anti-burnout guardrails (explicit)
1. Daily load capped at ~5 focused hours; the platform surfaces a warning if a student's self-reported time exceeds it consistently.
2. Spaced review replaces mass cramming — less total work for better retention.
3. Gates are re-sittable with no penalty, removing the high-stakes stress that drives burnout in exam-centric systems.
4. Weekly subject rotation prevents monotony and the cognitive fatigue of single-subject marathons.
