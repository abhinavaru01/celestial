# JEE Sample Problem Set — Rates & Motion

**Track:** Competitive (optional). **Level:** JEE-Main entry → standard.
**Linked core topics:** `math.F1.ratio-proportion-percentage`, `physics.F1.motion`.

> This set demonstrates the competitive section's structure (Phase 4 builds it out fully). Every problem links back to a core topic; a student who struggles is routed to that topic's **Common Mistakes** and **Tricks** layers. The point: competitive readiness is *depth + exam technique on top of core mastery*, not a separate curriculum.

## Strategy note (how these differ from board questions)
- JEE rewards **method economy** — the harmonic-mean shortcut for average speed, the $s \propto t^2$ ratio trick, and multiplier-chaining for percentages save decisive seconds.
- Questions **combine** ratio reasoning with kinematics in one step; board questions usually keep them separate. Interleaving in the core curriculum is exactly the preparation for this.
- Watch the classic traps (averaging speeds; adding successive percentages) — they are deliberately baited.

## Problems

**1. (Average speed / harmonic mean)** A particle covers equal distances with speeds 20, 30 and 60 m/s. Its average speed over the whole path is:
(a) 30 (b) 36 (c) 40 (d) 45 m/s
<details><summary>Solution</summary>
For equal distances, average speed = n / Σ(1/vᵢ) = 3 / (1/20 + 1/30 + 1/60) = 3 / (3/60 + 2/60 + 1/60) = 3 / (6/60) = 3 / 0.1 = **30 m/s. (a)** Uses the "total distance ÷ total time" rule from the ratio topic, generalised to three legs.
</details>

**2. ($s \propto t^2$ from rest)** A body starting from rest under uniform acceleration covers 20 m in the 1st two seconds. How far does it travel in the first 6 seconds?
(a) 60 m (b) 120 m (c) 180 m (d) 200 m
<details><summary>Solution</summary>
From rest, s ∝ t². s(6)/s(2) = 36/4 = 9, so s(6) = 9 × 20 = **180 m. (c)** Recognising the square-proportionality (a ratio idea) avoids computing a.
</details>

**3. (Percentage change, compounded)** A car's speed is increased by 25% and then decreased by 20%. For a fixed distance, the time taken changes by:
(a) no change (b) 5% less (c) 5% more (d) 25% less
<details><summary>Solution</summary>
Net speed factor = 1.25 × 0.80 = 1.00 → speed unchanged → time unchanged. **(a)** Successive percentages multiply, they don't add — the baited trap is choosing 5%.
</details>

**4. (Relative motion + ratio)** Two trains, lengths in ratio 2 : 3, move in opposite directions at 15 m/s and 25 m/s and cross each other in 5 s. Find the length of the shorter train.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>
Relative speed = 15 + 25 = 40 m/s. Total length crossed in 5 s = 40 × 5 = 200 m. Split 200 in ratio 2 : 3 → one part = 40 → shorter = 2 × 40 = **80 m.** Combines relative velocity (motion) with divide-in-ratio (ratio topic) — the interleaving JEE expects.
</details>

**5. (Reverse percentage / rate)** The price of petrol rises 20%. By what percentage must a driver reduce distance driven to keep fuel spending unchanged, assuming constant mileage? (nearest %)
<details><summary>Solution</summary>
Spending ∝ price × distance = const → inverse proportion. New distance factor = 1/1.2 = 0.833… → a reduction of **16.7% ≈ 17%.** Same reverse-multiplier move as the core topic's reverse-percentage problems.
</details>
