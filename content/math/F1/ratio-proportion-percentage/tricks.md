# Tricks & Problem-Solving Techniques — Ratio, Proportion & Percentage

### 1. The unitary method beats formulas
Whenever a proportion problem feels slippery, **find the value of one unit, then scale**. It's two safe steps and almost never misleads.
> 3 machines make 120 parts/hour → 1 machine makes 40 → 7 machines make 280. No equation needed.

### 2. Percentages you should just *know* (as fractions)
Converting to a friendly fraction turns hard mental arithmetic into easy division:

| % | Fraction | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 1/10 | move the decimal one place |
| 25% | 1/4 | divide by 4 |
| 50% | 1/2 | halve |
| 20% | 1/5 | divide by 5 |
| 33⅓% | 1/3 | divide by 3 |
| 12.5% | 1/8 | divide by 8 |

> 25% of 84 → just $84/4 = 21$. Faster and safer than $0.25\times84$.

### 3. Build any percentage from 10% and 5% and 1%
10% is trivial (move the decimal). 5% is half of that; 1% moves the decimal twice. Combine:
> 15% of 240 = 10% (24) + 5% (12) = **36**.
> 17% of 300 = 10% (30) + 5% (15) + 1%×2 (6) = **51**.

### 4. "% of" is commutative — flip it when it's easier
$p\%$ of $q$ = $q\%$ of $p$, because both equal $\frac{pq}{100}$.
> 8% of 50 is annoying; **50% of 8 = 4** is instant. Same answer.

### 5. Percentage change → multiplier
Turn every increase/decrease into a single **multiplier** and just multiply:
- increase by 20% → ×1.20
- decrease by 15% → ×0.85
> This makes successive changes trivial: up 20% then down 15% → $\times1.20\times0.85 = \times1.02$ → net +2%. No step-by-step needed.

### 6. Reverse percentage: name the multiplier, then divide
"After X% change the result is R." The original $= R \div (\text{multiplier})$.
> 480 after a 20% discount → multiplier 0.8 → original $= 480 / 0.8 = 600$.
> 660 after a 10% increase → original $= 660 / 1.1 = 600$.

### 7. Ratio → "one part" is the master key for sharing problems
For *any* "divide in ratio $a:b:c$" problem: total parts $= a+b+c$, one part $= \text{total}\div(a+b+c)$, then multiply. Works for 2, 3, or 10 shares identically.

### 8. Scale a recipe/map/model by keeping the ratio fixed
Any scaling problem (maps, recipes, similar shapes later in geometry) is "same ratio, different size." Set up $\frac{\text{known}_1}{\text{known}_2} = \frac{\text{want}}{\text{given}}$ and cross-multiply. This exact skill returns as **similarity** in F2 geometry.

### 9. Estimate first, compute second
Before calculating a percentage, bracket the answer: 23% of 480 is "a bit less than a quarter of 480 ≈ a bit less than 120." If your computed answer isn't near 110, you slipped a decimal. This estimation habit catches most careless errors and is a core exam skill.

### 10. Spot inverse proportion by the "fixed total work" cue
Words like *fixed job, same distance, same amount* signal inverse proportion — the **product** is constant. Immediately write $x_1y_1 = x_2y_2$ and you're done.
