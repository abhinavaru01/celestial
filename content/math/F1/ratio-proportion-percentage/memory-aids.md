# Memory & Retention Aids — Ratio, Proportion & Percentage

## Core anchors (the things to never forget)

- **"Ratio = division, not subtraction."** Comparing by "how many times," not "how much more."
- **"Per cent = per hundred."** The word *is* the definition: a fraction out of 100.
- **"% change: divide by where you started."** The original is the base, always.
- **"A rate is a ratio wearing units."** Speed, density, price — all ratios of unlike things.

## Mnemonics

- **PART + PART = WHOLE.** Before making any fraction of the total, add the parts. (Boys:Girls = 2:3 → whole is 2+3 = 5 parts.)
- **"OF means multiply, OFF means subtract."** 20% *of* 480 = 96; 20% *off* 480 = 480−96 = 384.
- **"Factors multiply, percentages don't add."** For successive changes, chain the multipliers (1.2 × 0.9), never add the percents.
- **"Reverse? Divide by the multiplier."** Result ÷ multiplier = original. (480 ÷ 0.8 = 600.)

## Visualization

- **The bar model.** Draw ratio parts as equal blocks. $2:3$ = two blocks and three blocks; the whole is five blocks. Shading blocks makes part-to-whole obvious and kills mistake #1.
- **The 100-grid for percentages.** Picture a 10×10 grid; a percentage is how many squares are shaded. 45% = 45 shaded squares. Instantly shows why percentages are "out of 100."
- **Number-line for proportion.** Direct proportion is a straight line through the origin (double x → double y); this picture *is* the graph you'll meet as $y = mx$ in F2 — same idea, so remembering one reinforces the other.

## Spaced-repetition prompts
_(The platform will resurface these at expanding intervals: 1 day → 3 → 7 → 16 → 35.)_

1. Split ₹540 in the ratio 4 : 5. (→ 240 and 300)
2. A jacket costs ₹850 after a 15% discount. Original price? (→ 850 ÷ 0.85 = ₹1000)
3. A quantity rises 25% then falls 20%. Net change? (→ ×1.25×0.80 = ×1.00 → no change)
4. 8 taps fill a tank in 9 hours. How long with 6 taps? (inverse → 8×9 = 6×t → 12 hours)
5. What is 35% of 60, done as "one third + a bit"? (→ 25% (15) + 10% (6) = 21)
6. Convert 5 m/s to km/h by scaling the ratio. (→ ×3600/1000 = 18 km/h) — *previews Physics F1 Motion.*

## The one-line recall card
> **Ratio compares by dividing; simplify by dividing; percentage is /100; change is over the original; reverse by dividing by the multiplier; a rate is a ratio with units — and that rate is where physics begins.**
