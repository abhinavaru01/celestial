# Vectors & 3-D Geometry

> [!intro] Vector algebra, dot and cross products, and lines and planes in three dimensions — the shared spine with physics.

## Vectors carry direction

A vector has magnitude and direction; you add them tip-to-tail and scale them by numbers. This formalises the scalar/vector idea met in F1 motion.

## Dot and cross products

The dot product measures alignment (and gives work in physics); the cross product gives a perpendicular vector (and torque). 3-D lines and planes are built from vectors.

```formula Key formulas
a·b = |a||b|cos θ = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃
|a×b| = |a||b|sin θ
a·b = 0 ⇔ perpendicular
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** Find the angle between a = (1, 0, 0) and b = (1, 1, 0).
> >
> > **Solution.** a·b = 1. |a| = 1, |b| = √2. cos θ = 1/√2 → θ = 45°.

## What you should be able to do

- Add, scale and resolve vectors
- Use the dot and cross products
- Describe lines and planes in 3-D
