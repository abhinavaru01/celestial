# Quadratics

> [!intro] Quadratic expressions and equations — factoring, the quadratic formula, graphs and modelling.

## Three ways to solve

A quadratic ax² + bx + c = 0 can be solved by factoring, completing the square, or the quadratic formula x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac))/2a. The discriminant b²−4ac tells how many real roots exist.

## The parabola

A quadratic graphs as a parabola. Its vertex is the turning point (a max or min), and the roots are where it crosses the x-axis — a picture that makes modelling intuitive.

```formula Key formulas
ax² + bx + c = 0 → x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac))/(2a)
Discriminant Δ = b² − 4ac: Δ>0 two roots, Δ=0 one, Δ<0 none (real)
Vertex at x = −b/(2a)
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** Solve x² − 4x − 5 = 0.
> >
> > **Solution.** Factor: (x − 5)(x + 1) = 0 → x = 5 or x = −1. (Check Δ = 16 + 20 = 36 > 0 → two real roots.)

## What you should be able to do

- Solve quadratics by factoring and the formula
- Sketch a parabola and find its vertex
- Model with quadratic equations
