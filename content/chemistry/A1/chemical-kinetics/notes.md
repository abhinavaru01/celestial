# Chemical Kinetics

> [!intro] Rate laws, order of reaction, half-life and the Arrhenius temperature dependence.

## Rate laws and order

Rate = k[A]^m[B]^n where m, n (the orders) are found by experiment, not from the equation. Overall order is m+n; units of k depend on the order.

## Arrhenius

k = A e^(−Eₐ/RT): rate constant rises with temperature and falls with activation energy. Logs give ln k = ln A − Eₐ/RT — a straight line (reusing logarithms).

```formula Key formulas
Rate = k[A]^m[B]^n  (m, n from experiment)
First-order half-life: t½ = 0.693 / k
Arrhenius: k = A e^(−Eₐ/RT);  ln k = ln A − Eₐ/RT
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** A first-order reaction has k = 0.0693 s⁻¹. Find its half-life.
> >
> > **Solution.** t½ = 0.693/k = 0.693/0.0693 = 10 s. First-order half-life is constant, independent of starting concentration.

## What you should be able to do

- Write and use rate laws
- Determine order experimentally
- Apply the Arrhenius relationship
