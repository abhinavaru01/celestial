# Ionic Equilibrium

> [!intro] pH of strong and weak acids, buffers (Henderson–Hasselbalch), and the solubility product Ksp.

## Weak acids and Ka

Strong acids dissociate fully; weak acids only partially, described by Ka. For a weak acid, [H⁺] ≈ √(Ka·C).

## Buffers and Ksp

A buffer (weak acid + its salt) resists pH change: pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]). The solubility product Ksp sets the maximum ionic product of a sparingly soluble salt.

```formula Key formulas
pH = −log[H⁺];  Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 10^(−14) at 25°C
Weak acid: [H⁺] ≈ √(Ka·C)
Henderson–Hasselbalch: pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA])
Ksp = [cation]^x[anion]^y at saturation
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** Find the pH of 0.10 M acetic acid (Ka = 1.8×10⁻⁵).
> >
> > **Solution.** [H⁺] ≈ √(Ka·C) = √(1.8×10⁻⁵ × 0.10) = √(1.8×10⁻⁶) ≈ 1.34×10⁻³. pH = −log(1.34×10⁻³) ≈ 2.87.
