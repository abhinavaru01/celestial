# Matter, Particles & States

> [!intro] The particle (kinetic-molecular) model of matter, the three states, and changes of state — the microscopic picture behind all of chemistry.

## Everything is particles in motion

All matter is made of tiny particles (atoms, molecules, ions) in constant motion. Their spacing, arrangement and energy decide the state: solids (fixed, vibrating), liquids (close, sliding), gases (far apart, fast, free).

## Changes of state are energy changes

Heating supplies energy that overcomes the forces holding particles together — melting then boiling. During a state change the temperature stays constant (energy goes into breaking forces, not raising KE). Mass is always conserved.

## Interparticle forces set properties

Stronger interparticle forces mean higher melting and boiling points. This single idea (developed further in bonding) explains why ionic solids melt high and gases condense low.

```formula Key formulas
Solid → Liquid → Gas : particle energy and spacing increase
During melting/boiling: temperature constant (latent heat breaks forces)
Mass conserved in every physical change
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** Why does the temperature stay constant while ice melts at 0°C even though heat is being added?
> >
> > **Solution.** The added energy (latent heat of fusion) breaks the forces holding water molecules in the rigid ice lattice, not raising their kinetic energy. Temperature (a measure of average KE) stays at 0°C until all the ice has melted.

## What you should be able to do

- Describe matter using the kinetic-particle model
- Explain the three states and changes between them by particle arrangement and energy
- Distinguish pure substances from mixtures
