# Physical vs Chemical Change

> [!intro] Telling physical from chemical changes, the evidence of reaction, and conservation of mass as the accounting law behind balancing.

## Two kinds of change

A physical change alters form but not the substance (melting, dissolving); a chemical change makes new substances (burning, rusting), often signalled by colour change, gas, heat/light, or a precipitate.

## Conservation of mass

Atoms are only rearranged in a reaction, so total mass of reactants = total mass of products. In an open system, escaping gas can make mass appear to fall — account for it.

```formula Key formulas
Total mass of reactants = total mass of products
mass(products) − mass(reactants) = 0 (closed system)
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** 4 g of hydrogen reacts completely with 32 g of oxygen. What mass of water forms?
> >
> > **Solution.** By conservation of mass, mass of water = 4 + 32 = 36 g. Atoms are only rearranged into H₂O.

## What you should be able to do

- Distinguish physical and chemical changes
- Identify signs of a chemical reaction
- Apply conservation of mass quantitatively
