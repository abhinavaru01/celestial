# Introductory Organic Chemistry

> [!intro] The chemistry of carbon: hydrocarbons, functional groups, homologous series and IUPAC naming.

## Carbon's versatility

Carbon forms four covalent bonds and chains/rings with itself (catenation), giving millions of compounds. Alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂) are saturated; alkenes have a C=C double bond.

## Functional groups & naming

A functional group (–OH, –COOH, C=C) is the reactive site defining a molecule's chemistry. IUPAC names use the longest carbon chain (meth-, eth-, prop-, but-) plus a suffix for the group.

```formula Key formulas
Alkanes: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (saturated, single bonds)
Alkenes: CₙH₂ₙ (one C=C double bond)
Chain roots: meth(1), eth(2), prop(3), but(4)
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** Give the molecular formula of the alkane with 3 carbons, and name it.
> >
> > **Solution.** CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ with n = 3 → C₃H₈, propane (a saturated hydrocarbon).

## What you should be able to do

- Recognise hydrocarbons and functional groups
- Name simple organic molecules (IUPAC)
- Explain carbon's versatility (catenation)
