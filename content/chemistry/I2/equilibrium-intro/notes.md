# Quantitative Equilibrium (Introduction)

> [!intro] Dynamic equilibrium, the equilibrium constant, and Le Chatelier's principle.

## Dynamic equilibrium

In a reversible reaction, equilibrium is reached when forward and backward rates are equal — concentrations stay constant though reaction continues.

## Le Chatelier

Disturb an equilibrium (concentration, temperature, pressure) and it shifts to oppose the change. Adding reactant or removing product shifts toward products.

```formula Key formulas
At equilibrium: forward rate = backward rate
For aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD :  K = [C]^c[D]^d / [A]^a[B]^b
Le Chatelier: system shifts to oppose the imposed change
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** For N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ (exothermic), how does increasing pressure affect NH₃ yield?
> >
> > **Solution.** Higher pressure shifts equilibrium to fewer gas moles (4 → 2), i.e., toward NH₃ — the basis of the Haber process.
