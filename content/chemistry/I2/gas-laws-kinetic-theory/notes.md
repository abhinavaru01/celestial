# Gas Laws & Kinetic Theory

> [!intro] Boyle's, Charles's and the combined/ideal gas laws, grounded in kinetic theory. Shared with physics.

## The gas laws

For a fixed amount of gas, P, V and T are linked: Boyle (PV constant at fixed T), Charles (V/T constant at fixed P), combined P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂. Adding amount gives PV = nRT.

## Kinetic theory

A gas is many particles in fast random motion; pressure is their wall collisions, and temperature (in kelvin) is proportional to average kinetic energy.

```formula Key formulas
PV = nRT,  R = 8.314 J/(mol·K)
Boyle: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ (const T)
Charles: V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂ (const P)
Combined: P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂ (T in kelvin)
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** A gas occupies 2.0 L at 300 K. What volume at 450 K, constant pressure?
> >
> > **Solution.** Charles: 2.0/300 = V₂/450 → V₂ = 2.0 × (450/300) = 3.0 L. (Use kelvin.)

## What you should be able to do

- Apply the individual and combined gas laws
- Use PV = nRT
- Relate macroscopic behaviour to particle motion
