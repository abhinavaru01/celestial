# The Mole Concept

> [!intro] The mole, Avogadro's number and molar mass — proportional reasoning applied to counting particles. The keystone of all quantitative chemistry.

## Counting by weighing

Atoms are too small to count, so we weigh them. One mole is Avogadro's number (Nₐ ≈ 6.022×10²³) of particles; the molar mass in g/mol equals the relative atomic/molecular mass, linking mass to particle count.

## The mole is a ratio

moles = mass ÷ molar mass — the same proportional reasoning as ratios, now applied to particles. Every stoichiometry, concentration and gas calculation flows from this.

```formula Key formulas
n = m / M   (moles = mass ÷ molar mass)
Number of particles = n × Nₐ,   Nₐ ≈ 6.022×10^23 /mol
Molar mass of a compound = Σ (atomic masses)
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** How many water molecules are in 36 g of water? (M = 18 g/mol)
> >
> > **Solution.** n = m/M = 36/18 = 2 mol. Molecules = n × Nₐ = 2 × 6.022×10²³ = 1.2×10²⁴ molecules.
