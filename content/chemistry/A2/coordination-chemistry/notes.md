# d-Block & Coordination Chemistry

> [!intro] Transition-metal properties, complex ions, ligands, coordination number and the origin of colour.

## Transition metals

d-block elements show variable oxidation states, coloured compounds and catalytic activity — all from partly filled d-orbitals.

## Complexes and colour

A central metal ion bonds to ligands (electron-pair donors); the number of donor atoms is the coordination number. Ligands split the d-orbitals in energy; electrons absorbing specific wavelengths to jump the gap produce colour.

```formula Key formulas
Coordination number = number of ligand donor atoms
Ligand = electron-pair (Lewis base) donor
Colour arises from d–d electron transitions (orbital splitting)
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** In [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺, what is the coordination number and why is it coloured?
> >
> > **Solution.** Six water ligands → coordination number 6. They split Cu²⁺'s 3d orbitals; a d-electron absorbs part of visible light to jump the gap, so the complex appears blue.
