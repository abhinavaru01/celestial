# Organic Reaction Mechanisms

> [!intro] Nucleophiles, electrophiles, curly-arrow mechanisms and the major pathways — reasoned, not memorised.

## Electron flow

Organic reactions are electrons moving from electron-rich (nucleophile) to electron-poor (electrophile) sites. Curly arrows show electron-pair movement, always starting from a lone pair or bond.

## Major pathways

Nucleophilic substitution (SN1/SN2), electrophilic addition (to C=C), and elimination. Understanding the pathway predicts products instead of memorising thousands of reactions.

```formula Key formulas
Curly arrow: starts from an electron pair (lone pair or bond)
Nucleophile = electron-pair donor (electron-rich)
Electrophile = electron-pair acceptor (electron-poor)
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** In OH⁻ + CH₃Br, identify the nucleophile and product.
> >
> > **Solution.** OH⁻ is the electron-rich nucleophile; it attacks the electron-poor carbon bonded to Br, displacing Br⁻ (nucleophilic substitution). Product: CH₃OH.

## What you should be able to do

- Identify nucleophiles and electrophiles
- Follow substitution/addition/elimination mechanisms
- Predict products by electron flow
