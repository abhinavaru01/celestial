# Solutions & Concentration

> [!intro] Molarity, dilution and the mole concept applied to real solutions.

## Concentration as a rate

Molarity (M) is moles of solute per litre of solution — again a ratio, bridging the mole concept to practical lab quantities.

## Dilution conserves moles

Adding solvent lowers concentration but keeps moles of solute fixed: C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ — inverse proportion in action.

```formula Key formulas
Molarity M = n / V(litres)
n = M × V
Dilution: C₁V₁ = C₂V₂
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** What volume of 2 M stock makes 250 mL of 0.5 M solution?
> >
> > **Solution.** C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ → 2 × V₁ = 0.5 × 0.250 → V₁ = 0.0625 L = 62.5 mL, then dilute to 250 mL.

## What you should be able to do

- Calculate molarity
- Perform dilution calculations
- Relate concentration, moles and volume
