# Bonding I

> [!intro] Ionic, covalent and metallic bonding from the drive for full outer shells, and how bonding type dictates properties.

## Atoms bond for stable octets

Atoms gain, lose or share electrons to reach a full outer shell. Metal + non-metal transfer → ionic; non-metals share → covalent; metals pool a "sea" of delocalised electrons → metallic.

## Bonding sets properties

Ionic solids form giant lattices (high melting, conduct when molten/aqueous); covalent molecules are often low-melting and non-conducting; metals conduct and are malleable via mobile electrons.

```formula Key formulas
Ionic: metal (loses e⁻) + non-metal (gains e⁻) → ions
Covalent: shared electron pair between non-metals
Metallic: cations in a sea of delocalised electrons
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** Predict the bonding and a property of MgCl₂.
> >
> > **Solution.** Mg transfers 2 electrons to two Cl → Mg²⁺ and 2Cl⁻: ionic. Property: high melting point; conducts when molten/dissolved (mobile ions).

## What you should be able to do

- Distinguish ionic, covalent, metallic bonds
- Draw electron-dot (Lewis) structures
- Relate bonding to physical properties
