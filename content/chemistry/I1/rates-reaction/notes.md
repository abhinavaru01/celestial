# Rates of Reaction

> [!intro] Collision theory and the factors that change reaction rate, read from rate graphs.

## Collision theory

Reactions occur when particles collide with energy ≥ activation energy and correct orientation. Concentration, temperature, surface area and catalysts increase the frequency or success of collisions.

## Rate from graphs

Rate = Δ[concentration] ÷ time = the gradient of a concentration-vs-time graph — steep at first, flattening as reactants deplete. Rate-as-slope, shared with physics and calculus.

```formula Key formulas
Rate = Δ[concentration] / Δt = gradient of the curve
Higher T, [conc], surface area, or a catalyst → faster
Catalyst lowers activation energy Eₐ (not consumed)
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** A reaction produces 20 cm³ of gas in the first 10 s, then slows. Average rate over that interval?
> >
> > **Solution.** Average rate = 20 ÷ 10 = 2 cm³/s. It slows later as reactant concentration falls, reducing collision frequency.
