# Memory & Retention Aids — Motion

## Core anchors

- **"Speed is a rate — it's the ratio topic in a lab coat."** Distance ÷ time, a ratio of unlike quantities.
- **"Average speed = total distance ÷ total time."** Never the plain average of two speeds.
- **"Acceleration is the rate of a rate."** How fast velocity changes.
- **"Slope is the rate; area is the amount."** The two-phrase key to every motion graph.

## Mnemonics

- **"3.6 goes up, 3.6 comes down."** ×3.6 for m/s → km/h, ÷3.6 for km/h → m/s.
- **SUVAT** — the five kinematics symbols: **s** (displacement), **u** (initial velocity), **v** (final velocity), **a** (acceleration), **t** (time). List them, fill three, find the fourth.
- **"Don't lose the half"** — $s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2$. The ½ is the triangle under the v–t graph.
- **"Scalar = size, Vector = size + direction."** Distance/speed are scalars; displacement/velocity/acceleration are vectors.

## Visualization

- **The v–t graph is your calculator.** Any uniform-acceleration problem = a trapezium. Height = velocity, slope = acceleration, area = distance. Draw it and read off answers; you rarely need to *remember* the equations because the picture regenerates them.
- **The round-trip picture.** Walk out and back: the path (distance) is long, but the arrow from start to finish (displacement) is zero. Fixes the scalar/vector confusion permanently.
- **Top-of-the-throw freeze-frame.** At the peak, the ball is momentarily still (v = 0) but gravity is still pulling (a = g ≠ 0) — which is exactly why it drops. Cements "v = 0 does not mean a = 0."

## Spaced-repetition prompts
_(Resurfaced at 1 → 3 → 7 → 16 → 35 days.)_

1. Convert 90 km/h to m/s. (→ 90 ÷ 3.6 = 25 m/s)
2. A car goes 0 → 30 m/s in 6 s. Acceleration? (→ 5 m/s²)
3. On a velocity–time graph, what does the slope represent? The area? (→ acceleration; distance)
4. A stone dropped from rest falls for 3 s. Speed just before landing (g = 10)? (→ 30 m/s)
5. You run 400 m around a track back to the start in 80 s. Average speed? Average velocity? (→ 5 m/s; 0, since displacement is 0)
6. Which equation has no time in it? (→ v² = u² + 2as)
7. Why is a car rounding a bend at constant speed still accelerating? (→ direction of velocity changes)

## The one-line recall card
> **Speed is a rate (a ratio with units); velocity and displacement carry direction; acceleration is the rate of change of velocity; on graphs slope = rate and area = distance; for constant a use SUVAT — and don't lose the ½.**
