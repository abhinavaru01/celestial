# Circular Motion & Gravitation

> [!intro] Centripetal acceleration, orbits and Newton's law of gravitation.

## Centripetal force

Circular motion at constant speed still accelerates — toward the centre. A net centripetal force (mv²/r) is required; remove it and the object flies off tangentially.

## Gravitation

Every mass attracts every other with F = Gm₁m₂/r². This same force keeps planets in orbit, balancing the centripetal requirement.

```formula Key formulas
Centripetal acceleration a = v²/r (toward centre)
Centripetal force F = mv²/r
Gravitation F = Gm₁m₂/r² (inverse-square)
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** If the distance between two masses triples, how does the gravitational force change?
> >
> > **Solution.** F ∝ 1/r². Tripling r gives F × 1/3² = 1/9 of the original force.
