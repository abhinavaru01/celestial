# Argument I

> [!intro] Claim, evidence and warrant — recognising the structure of arguments.

## The parts of an argument

An argument makes a claim, backs it with evidence, and connects the two with a warrant (the reasoning). Naming these parts lets you analyse any argument.

## Evaluating support

Good arguments have relevant, sufficient evidence and sound reasoning. Spotting weak links is the core of critical thinking — and of science data-literacy.

```formula Key techniques
Argument = claim + evidence + warrant (reasoning)
Evidence must be relevant and sufficient
Find the unstated assumption
```

> [!example] **Worked example**
> **Problem.** Identify the parts: "We should sleep 8 hours (claim) because studies link sleep to memory (evidence)." What is the warrant?
> >
> > **Solution.** The warrant (unstated) is that better memory is desirable and that the studies apply to us.

## What you should be able to do

- Identify claim, evidence and warrant
- Analyse an argument's structure
- Evaluate whether evidence supports a claim
